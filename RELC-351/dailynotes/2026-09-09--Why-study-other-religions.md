## Holy Envy

Why study other religions?

"***Holy Envy***": Seeing something from another religion that you wish was a part of yours.

Three rules for good interfaith dialogue (by the guy who coined the term "holy envy").

1. Don't compare your best to their worst. Compare best to best.
2. If you want to know what someone believes or what a religion teaches, don't ask an antagonist, non-member, or ex-member. Ask someone currently practicing.
3. Always leave room for "holy envy". If you can't see something worthy of emulation or liking, you either know nothing about the religion or are so close minded there's no point in learning about the religion anyway.

## Gaskill's story

Gaskill shared why he has dedicated his life to studying other religions:

- He's a convert (to the Mormon church).
  - Gaskill grew up Greek Orthodox, one of the few Christian denominations still claiming to be the one true church. He knows the consequence of never exploring other religions&mdash;had he not, he never would have found Christ's restored gospel.
- Avoiding *faux pas*. Gaskill wants to know enough to not commit any big social missteps.
  - e.g., a person presenting the Sikh's golden temple asked "What does this remind you of?" and a (LDS) shouted, "the great & spacious building!"
    - Gaskill: Why would you think that? And if you do, why would yo usay it out loud?
  - Gaskill dressed as religious figures for many Halloweens. One time, he dressed up as a Jew on a Halloween that fell on a Saturday, the Jewish sabbath.
- You're inevitably going to meet peoplle from other religions.
  - "Building bridges...is essential because we often find ourselves in social or family relationships with those whose traditions are unfamiliar to us" - some smart guy or smth idk.
- Gaskill served in a religious melting pot. He said his unfamiliarty sometimes made it hard to connect w/ folks.
  - (London, speaking Nigerian.)
